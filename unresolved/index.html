Our method, in order

What has to fail before a match is published as unresolved

  1. Featured exact-name market — Polymarket series-winner order books, both sides, fresh and inside the spread ceiling.
  2. Other first-party exchanges and books — Kalshi match-winner books, Thunderpick paired prices, Bovada and Pinnacle series moneylines.
  3. Relayed bookmaker quote — a two-sided price published by bo3.gg, labelled as a relay and only used when no first-party quote exists.
  4. Aggregated reference — a dated, reviewed bookmaker-average capture.
  5. Valve official rating model — complete five-player rows from Valve’s published regional standings; uncalibrated, labelled.
  6. Own results model — a Bradley–Terry fit over finished bo3.gg series, anchored on Valve points, failing closed on thin graphs.
  7. HLTV world-ranking model — the published weekly points through a 400-scale logistic transform; unranked sides get HLTV’s floor and an explicit low-confidence label.
  8. Wide-book guidance — a midpoint of a book too wide to accept, badged as guidance and never counted as money-backed.
  9. Unresolved — the honest blank, with the blocker and the sources tried.

When a research model lands on exactly 50/50, the pick is set by the ranking provider’s own published order (for example HLTV rank 215 vs 229) and the row says so. That rule changes the direction only — never a probability.
